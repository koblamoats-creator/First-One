    How to actually get recruited

    A short, no-fluff playbook — the moves that actually landed recent players at D2, D3, NAIA, and JUCO programs. You don't need money or connections. You need these steps, done consistently.

    1 Get an honest evaluation before anything else

    • Ask a coach or trainer who isn't family: "What level am I realistically?" — then believe the answer.
    • Cheapest honest read: go to a college's own ID camp. You get evaluated by that exact staff, and it's a fraction of the cost of a recruiting service. Ask about a camp fee waiver if money's tight — many say yes.

    2 Make film coaches will actually watch

    • A phone and a free editor (or Hudl) is enough. You do not need a paid mixtape.
    • 3–5 minutes, best plays first — coaches stop watching after 30 seconds if it's slow.
    • Put your jersey color/number on the opening screen and spotlight yourself on each clip.
    • Show more than dunks: defense, rebounding, a clean pass, a catch-and-shoot. Role players get recruited.
    • Add one full-game link so coaches can see you in flow.

    3 Email coaches directly — the right way

    • Email the coach yourself (parents in the background). Coaches want to hear from the player.
    • Cast a wide net: 30–50 realistic-level programs, not just dream schools. D2, D3, and NAIA coaches reply the most.
    • Keep it short. Put the key facts in the first two lines and the film link up high.
    • Follow up in ~2 weeks if no reply — persistence is normal and expected.
    Copy-and-personalize email template
    Subject: 2026 G/F — 6'2" — [Your Name], [GPA] GPA — highlight film

Coach [Last Name],

I'm [Name], a [position] at [High School] ([City, State]),
class of [year]. I'm very interested in [College] and your program.

- Height / position: 6'2" guard
- GPA: 3.4  |  SAT/ACT: [score or "test-optional"]
- Team: [varsity role] | Club: [AAU team]
- Film: [link]
- My schedule: [next few game dates/locations]

I'd love to be on your radar and hear what you look for.
Thank you for your time.

[Name] | [phone] | [email]

    4 Play where you'll be seen

    • You don't need the most expensive AAU team. You need events college coaches attend — ask target coaches which camps/showcases they'll be at, and go to those.
    • One well-chosen college ID camp beats a season of tournaments no coach watches.
    • Get your high-school or AAU coach to make a call for you — a coach's word carries real weight.

    5 Win in the classroom — it's recruiting currency

    • Register early with the NCAA Eligibility Center and stay on your 16 core courses.
    • Grades are money: at D3 they're your only "scholarship," and everywhere they unlock merit aid.
    • A strong transcript makes a coach's job easier — sometimes it's the tiebreaker for the last roster spot.

    6 Be easy to recruit

    • Reply to coaches fast and politely. Keep a simple spreadsheet of who you've contacted.
    • Clean up your social media, then use it: post clips and tag programs.
    • Coaches recruit character. Hustle, good body language, and being coachable get noticed on film and in person.
    💬 What recently-recruited players say worked: "I emailed 40 D2/D3 coaches myself — the three that replied were schools I'd never heard of, and one became my offer." · "The college's own camp got me evaluated honestly and on their board." · "My AAU coach made one phone call and it opened the door faster than months of emails."

    Quick timeline

    • 9th–10th: train, make film, get an honest evaluation, keep grades up.
    • 11th: register with the NCAA, email coaches widely, attend ID camps — junior year is prime time.
    • 12th: follow up hard, take visits, compare net-price calculators, commit to the best fit.
